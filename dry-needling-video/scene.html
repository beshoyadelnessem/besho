Posterior rotator cuff

  • Supraspinatus — supraspinous fossa → greater tubercle (superior facet). Initiates abduction, centres the humeral head.
  • Infraspinatus — infraspinous fossa → greater tubercle (middle facet). External rotation.
  • Teres minor — lateral border → greater tubercle (inferior facet). External rotation.
  • Nerves: suprascapular (C5–6) for supra- & infraspinatus; axillary for teres minor.

Referred pain pattern

  • Deep ache over the mid-deltoid region
  • May spill down the lateral arm
  • Often concentrates at the lateral epicondyle
  • Aggravated by abduction; dull ache at rest / night
Travell, Simons & Simons' Trigger Point Manual, 3rd ed.

Palpation

  • Patient prone, arm relaxed by the side
  • Trace the spine of scapula to the acromion; find the superior border & superior angle
  • Flat palpation in the fossa, across the fibres
  • Taut band + tender spot that reproduces the patient's familiar pain

Response & aftercare

  • Local twitch response (LTR) confirms the trigger point
  • Proposed effects: ↓ muscle tension, ↑ local blood flow, ↓ nociceptive input
  • Post-needling soreness for 24–48 h is common
  • Re-assess pain & abduction range immediately

Supraspinatus dry needling — key points

Prone, arm by the side
Mid-belly, 1–2 cm above the spine of scapula
Perpendicular, aimed caudally to the fossa floor
Bone is the backstop
Stay away from the suprascapular notch
Never angle anteriorly over the superior border
⚠ Educational use only — practise only after accredited training, under supervision.
